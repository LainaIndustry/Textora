How to Count Words in Microsoft Word

By the Textora Editorial Team Approximately 900 words About 5 minutes to read

Word has accurate built-in word counts, but the details matter. This guide shows you where to find them and what they include.

The Status Bar

Word displays a live word count at the bottom-left of the document window. The count updates as you type. If you select text, the status bar shows the selection count in the format "X of Y words," where Y is the whole document.

If the word count is missing from the status bar, right-click the status bar and enable "Word Count" in the menu.

The Word Count Dialog

To open the detailed dialog:

  1. Click Review on the ribbon.
  2. Select Word Count.
  3. A dialog shows pages, words, characters (with and without spaces), paragraphs and lines.

You can also press Ctrl + Shift + G (Windows) or Cmd + Shift + G (Mac) as a shortcut, depending on Word version.

Counting a Selection

Select text, then open the dialog. It shows the selection's word count and other metrics alongside the whole document. This is useful for counting a specific chapter or section.

What Word Counts

  • Body text: included.
  • Headings: included.
  • Footnotes and endnotes: included when the option is enabled.
  • Text boxes: included when "Include textboxes, footnotes and endnotes" is checked in the dialog.
  • Headers and footers: not included in the body count.
  • Comments: not counted in the main body.

The dialog has a checkbox that controls inclusion of textboxes, footnotes and endnotes. Check it to get a complete count if your document uses these.

Character Counts

The dialog shows characters with and without spaces. This matters for platforms and forms with character limits. Word counts characters including spaces by default; the without-spaces figure is shown separately.

Counting Paragraphs and Lines

The dialog also reports paragraph and line counts. This is useful for structure checks: for example, checking average sentences per paragraph, or whether a document has excessively long paragraphs. Combine these numbers with the Paragraph Counter for a second opinion.

Non-Latin Text and Special Characters

Word uses its own tokenization rules, which vary by language settings. Chinese, Japanese and Thai text may count differently from Latin text. Emoji and symbols may or may not count as words depending on Word's version and settings. For precise counts in these scripts, an external tool can help confirm.

When to Use an External Tool

Word's built-in count covers the basics but doesn't calculate reading time, unique word count, keyword density or sentence statistics. Use the Textora Word Counter when you need:

  • Reading time estimates.
  • Keyword frequency analysis.
  • Sentence and paragraph statistics.
  • A second opinion on a count near a limit.

Copy the text into the tool, then compare counts. Small differences between Word and other tools are normal.

Staying Under a Limit

If you have a word limit, keep the status bar count visible and check it periodically while writing. Set a target slightly under the limit to allow for edits and citations. If you're using footnotes, check whether they count in your assignment, then configure the dialog accordingly.

Conclusion

Word's status bar and word count dialog provide accurate counts for most needs. For deeper analysis, use an external tool. The Textora Word Counter adds reading time, unique words and keyword frequency that Word does not provide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Word count footnotes?

Yes, if the "Include textboxes, footnotes and endnotes" option is checked in the word count dialog.

How do I count only part of a document?

Select the text, then open the word count dialog. It shows both the selection and the total.

Does Word count headers and footers?

Not in the body count. If they need to be included, copy them into the body or count them separately.

Why does Word's count differ from another tool?

Different tools apply different tokenization rules for punctuation, non-Latin scripts and emoji. Small differences are expected.
