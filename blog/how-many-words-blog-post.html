How Many Words Should a Blog Post Have?

By the Textora Editorial Team Approximately 1,000 words About 5 minutes to read

The honest answer is: as many as the topic needs and no more. Still, length ranges help set expectations. This guide breaks down common post types, why length varies, and how to decide when a draft is done.

Why There Is No Single Number

Blog post length depends on the question you are answering, the depth your audience needs, and the format you've chosen. A recipe can be 400 words and complete. A technical explainer might need 2,500. A news brief might be 300. Choosing a single "ideal" length for every post ignores the reader's needs and the topic's complexity.

Common Length Ranges by Post Type

  • News briefs and announcements: 200–500 words.
  • How-to guides: 800–1,500 words.
  • Listicles: 800–2,000 words depending on the number of items.
  • Opinion pieces: 600–1,200 words.
  • Technical deep dives: 1,500–3,000+ words.
  • Pillar or hub pages: 2,000–5,000 words, often linking to shorter posts.

These ranges are starting points, not rules. Your topic and audience decide.

What Length Signals

Length alone does not determine quality, but it correlates with depth. A 2,000-word post has room for background, examples, edge cases and FAQs. A 500-word post cannot cover the same ground. For readers, length signals commitment: longer content may be more thorough, but it also asks more of them.

At the same time, padding to hit a length target backfires. Readers skim past filler and lose trust. A tight 900-word post usually outperforms a padded 1,500-word one.

How to Decide Your Length

  1. Start with the reader's question. What does the reader actually need to know?
  2. Outline the answer. Count the sections. Estimate how many paragraphs each requires.
  3. Pick a format. A guide, a list, a case study or a news brief each carry expectations.
  4. Look at competing resources. If every resource on the topic is 3,000 words and you plan 400, ask whether yours covers what readers expect. If it does, shorter is fine.
  5. Write the draft, then check the word count. Don't stop mid-paragraph to hit a number.
  6. Cut what doesn't serve the answer. Editors can often remove 10–20% without losing meaning.

Reading Time and Length

Reading time scales with word count at roughly the same WPM. A 500-word post is around 2 minutes; a 1,500-word post is around 6 minutes. The Reading Time Calculator can help you estimate how much commitment a post asks of readers.

When Shorter Is Better

  • The answer is simple and unambiguous.
  • The audience is on mobile and expects quick takes.
  • The post is part of a series where each article covers one slice.
  • The content is news, and readers want the facts quickly.

When Longer Is Justified

  • The topic has many subtopics or edge cases.
  • Readers are research-oriented and expect thoroughness.
  • The post is a pillar page meant to be a reference.
  • The topic is complex and needs examples and context.

Editing for Length

If a draft runs long, look for:

  • Repeated points stated in different words.
  • Examples that support minor points.
  • Lengthy introductions or background that delays the answer.
  • Unnecessary transitions and hedging language.

If a draft runs short, ask whether the reader's question is fully answered. Adding an example, a definition or a short FAQ is usually better than padding with generalities.

Word Count Isn't a Ranking Guarantee

Longer content is sometimes associated with higher search rankings, but the relationship is not a rule. What matters is whether the content satisfies the reader's intent. A well-structured 800-word answer to a specific question can outperform a 3,000-word post that wanders. Length is a tool for serving the reader, not a target in itself.

Conclusion

Pick a length that serves your topic and audience. Use rough ranges as starting points, not targets. Write the full answer, then edit for clarity and length together. The Word Counter and Reading Time Calculator help you see what your draft asks of readers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is longer content always better for SEO?

No. Content should match search intent. A focused, complete answer is more valuable than padding.

How long should a beginner's guide be?

Often 1,000–2,000 words. Enough to explain the topic without overwhelming a new reader.

Can a blog post be too short?

Yes, if it fails to answer the reader's question or omits necessary context. Length should serve completeness.

Does reading time affect rankings?

Reading time is a user-facing metric, not a direct ranking signal.
