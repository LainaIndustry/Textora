How to Calculate Reading Time

By the Textora Editorial Team Approximately 1,000 words About 5 minutes to read

Reading time estimates help editors plan content, help readers decide whether to commit, and help presenters judge whether a written script fits their slot. This guide explains the calculation and how to adjust it for real-world conditions.

The Basic Formula

Reading time is calculated by dividing the word count by a words-per-minute (WPM) rate:

Reading time (minutes) = Word count ÷ Words per minute

For a 1,200-word article at 240 WPM, reading time is 5 minutes. For a 300-word post at 240 WPM, it is 1.25 minutes, or about 1 minute and 15 seconds.

What WPM Rate Should You Use?

Reading rates vary widely by material and reader. Commonly cited ranges:

  • Very light content (short news, casual posts): 250–300 WPM.
  • Standard articles and reports: 220–250 WPM.
  • Technical or dense material: 150–200 WPM.
  • Careful reading or study: often below 150 WPM.

Textora defaults to 238 WPM, a commonly used figure for adult native speakers reading standard prose. The Reading Time Calculator lets you change this rate.

Why Reading Time Is Only an Estimate

Real reading speed depends on factors the formula does not capture:

  • Subject familiarity: experts read technical content faster than newcomers.
  • Language: reading in a second language is typically slower.
  • Device: screen size and font rendering affect reading pace.
  • Formatting: dense paragraphs, tables and footnotes slow readers down.
  • Purpose: skimming, studying and reading aloud have different rates.

Treat the result as a planning heuristic, not a precise prediction.

Reading Time for Different Content Types

Blog Posts

Most blog posts fall between 500 and 2,000 words. At 238 WPM, that's roughly 2 to 8.5 minutes. Readers often skim blog posts, so the displayed reading time can feel longer than the actual time spent. Displaying a reading time still helps readers gauge commitment.

News Articles

News articles are often shorter and more direct. A 500-word piece reads in about 2 minutes. Because news readers typically skim, reading time estimates for news tend to overestimate time spent.

Technical Documentation

Technical docs include code snippets, tables and diagrams. The text-only word count misses time spent interpreting these. For technical content, consider a lower WPM (around 150–180) to account for comprehension time.

Books and Long Form

Books vary by genre. A 90,000-word novel at 240 WPM is about 6.25 hours of pure reading—but most readers take longer due to breaks, rereading and life in between. Long-form reading time is best framed in hours, not minutes.

How to Calculate It

  1. Get a word count. Use the Word Counter or your word processor's built-in count.
  2. Choose a WPM rate. Match the rate to the material type.
  3. Divide words by WPM. The result is minutes.
  4. Round sensibly. Displaying "5 min read" is clearer than "4 min 48 sec read" for most readers.
  5. Adjust if needed. For technical or dense material, lower the WPM.

Reading Time vs Speaking Time

Reading is typically faster than speaking. A script read silently might take 5 minutes; read aloud at a natural pace, the same text may take 7–8 minutes. For presentations, use the Speaking Time Calculator with a lower WPM rate (often 130–160 WPM).

Showing Reading Time to Users

Displayed reading time should be rounded and approximate. "5 min read" is friendlier than "4.8 minutes." Avoid overly precise displays unless your audience expects them. Also avoid framing reading time as a ranking signal—it is a user-facing signal of length, not a search engine signal.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a single WPM for all content. Dense material reads slower.
  • Ignoring formatting. Long paragraphs slow readers down.
  • Overpromising accuracy. Reading time is an estimate, not a measurement.
  • Forgetting images and interactivity. These add real reading time that text-only counts miss.

Conclusion

Reading time is a simple division problem with a lot of real-world nuance. Choose a WPM rate that matches your content, round the result for display, and treat it as a planning tool rather than a promise. The Reading Time Calculator lets you test different rates in seconds.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average reading speed?

Many sources cite around 200–250 WPM for adult readers of standard prose. Technical or dense material is often read more slowly.

Should I count only the body text?

For a reading time estimate, count the text a typical reader will actually read. Exclude navigation, footers and advertisements. Include headings and captions.

Does reading time include looking at images?

Not in a text-based calculation. Images and diagrams add time that a word count cannot measure.

Is a shorter reading time better?

Not inherently. Length should match the subject and the reader's needs. Reading time helps set expectations, not judge quality.
