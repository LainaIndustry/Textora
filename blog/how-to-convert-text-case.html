How to Convert Text to Uppercase or Lowercase

By the Textora Editorial Team Approximately 950 words About 5 minutes to read

Case affects tone, readability and consistency. This guide explains the four common case styles and how to convert text quickly without breaking formatting.

The Four Common Case Styles

  • UPPERCASE: all capital letters.
  • lowercase: all lowercase letters.
  • Title Case: the first letter of each word is capitalized.
  • Sentence case: only the first letter of each sentence is capitalized.

Each has its place. Mixing them inconsistently is one of the most visible signs of unpolished writing.

When to Use Uppercase

  • Acronyms and abbreviations: NASA, HTML, PDF.
  • Short warnings or notices: DANGER, PLEASE READ.
  • Labels and short headings where emphasis matters.
  • Codes and identifiers where case is significant.

Uppercase is harder to read in long passages. Reserve it for short text.

When to Use Lowercase

  • Body text in most style guides.
  • Usernames and hashtags on many platforms.
  • Brand style choices (e.g., all-lowercase brand names).
  • Data normalization before comparison.

Lowercase body text is easier to read than uppercase, especially on screens.

Title Case: Rules and Variations

Title case capitalizes the first letter of most words. Different style guides disagree on which short words to leave lowercase. Common conventions lowercase:

  • Articles: a, an, the.
  • Short conjunctions: and, but, or.
  • Short prepositions: in, on, at, of, to.

Most also capitalize the first and last word of the title regardless of part of speech. Our Title Case Converter uses a simple rule (capitalize every word) that you can adjust manually. Check your publication's style guide before publishing.

Sentence Case: The Standard for Body Text

Sentence case capitalizes the first letter of each sentence and leaves everything else lowercase, except proper nouns and other standard exceptions. It is the default for body text in most style guides.

Automatic sentence-case converters use punctuation rules and may incorrectly capitalize after abbreviations like “Dr.” or “e.g.” Always review the result.

How to Convert Text in Your Browser

  1. Paste your text into the Text Case Converter.
  2. Choose the desired case style.
  3. Press convert.
  4. Review the result for abbreviations, acronyms and proper nouns that should stay in their original case.
  5. Copy the result back into your document.

For single-style conversion, use the dedicated Uppercase, Lowercase or Title Case tools.

What Case Conversion Does Not Change

  • Punctuation.
  • Numbers.
  • Spacing and line breaks.
  • Some accented or non-Latin characters, depending on the script.

Common Problems

Broken acronyms

Title case converts “NASA” to “Nasa.” Add a manual pass to restore acronyms.

Capitalized prepositions

Simple title case capitalizes every word. Apply your style guide afterward if small words should be lowercase.

Abbreviation mis-capitalization

Sentence case may capitalize after “Dr.” or “e.g.” because it sees a period. Review and correct manually.

Loss of emphasis

Converting text to lowercase removes intentional capitalization used for emphasis. Reapply emphasis with formatting (bold, italic) instead.

Case and Accessibility

Long passages in uppercase are harder to read for many users, including those with dyslexia. Reserve uppercase for short labels and warnings. For emphasis, use bold or italic rather than all caps.

Conclusion

Case conversion is a quick way to normalize text, but it benefits from a review pass. Use the Text Case Converter for most jobs, and check acronyms, proper nouns and abbreviations before publishing. Consistency matters more than any single rule.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does case conversion affect punctuation?

No. Punctuation, numbers and spacing are preserved.

Will title case capitalize “and” and “the”?

Our simple title case capitalizes every word. You can manually lowercase short words after conversion to match your style guide.

Does uppercase conversion handle accented letters?

Yes. JavaScript's toUpperCase handles most accented characters, though some scripts do not have a case distinction.

Can I convert only part of the text?

Yes. Paste only the portion you want to convert, then copy the result back into the full document.
