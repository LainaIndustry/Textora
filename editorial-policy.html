Editorial Policy

Effective date: 6 November 2026

This page describes how the articles and tool instructions on Textora are created, reviewed and updated. It is written so that readers can judge how much weight to give the content on this site.

1. How the Site Is Operated

Textora is an independent website operated by a single publisher. It is not part of a company, media organization or editorial team. The same person who builds and maintains the tools also writes the articles and tool instructions. There is no separate editorial board, no external fact-checking team, and no paid contributors.

This transparency matters because it affects how the content should be read: articles reflect the operator's own experience and research, not a formal institutional review process.

2. How Articles Are Created

Each article is written with a specific reader question in mind. The typical workflow is:

  1. Identify the question. Choose a task or concept the site's tools are designed to support.
  2. Research and outline. Gather what the tools actually do, read the relevant platform documentation if the article discusses an external tool, and outline the answer.
  3. Draft with examples. Write the article with practical examples rather than general descriptions.
  4. Test claims against the site. Any count, transformation or behavior described in an article is checked against how the tool on this site actually works.
  5. Edit for clarity and length. Cut repetition and tighten language before publishing.
  6. Add internal links. Link to the relevant tools and to related articles so readers can move from reading to doing.

3. How Tool Instructions Are Maintained

Tool pages on Textora describe what the tool does, how to use it, common use cases and known limitations. When tool behavior changes, the descriptions are updated to match. Any statement about accuracy, privacy or calculation method is reviewed against the current implementation of the tool.

If a tool's limitations are unclear or a count can vary between tools, the page says so rather than overstating accuracy.

4. Originality

All articles and tool instructions on this site are written for this site. They are not copied from competitors, generated automatically without review, or spun from other sources. Where the site refers to an external tool, platform or standard, it does so in its own words and links to the original source where useful.

The site does not publish fabricated statistics, references, quotes, testimonials or author names.

5. Accuracy and Limitations

Content is written to be accurate as of its publication date. Two practical limitations apply:

  • External platforms change. Articles that describe Word, Google Docs or search engine behavior may become out of date if those platforms change. The site attempts to describe general principles rather than fragile interface details, but some specifics may drift.
  • Text metrics have inherent variation. Word counts, character counts and reading times are mechanical measurements. They are explained accurately, but the site does not claim that its results will match every other tool.

Where a claim cannot be verified, the article says so instead of presenting an estimate as fact.

6. Corrections

Errors are corrected when they are identified. Small typographical or formatting fixes may be made silently. Substantive corrections — changes that affect the meaning, the accuracy of a claim, or the instructions — are noted where practical in the article itself.

If you find an error, report it through the Contact page or by emailing textora1counter@gmail.com. Reports are read and, where the issue is confirmed, fixed.

7. Updates

Articles may be updated to reflect changes in tools, corrections from readers, or improvements in clarity. Pages do not display invented publication dates. Where a date is shown, it reflects the most recent substantive work on the page rather than the first time the topic was mentioned on the site.

8. Author Attribution

Articles on Textora are published without named bylines. This is intentional. The site does not present fictional authors, invented credentials or editorial titles. Where an article refers to "the site operator" or "Textora," it means the single individual who runs the site.

If a named author is ever added in the future, that person's real identity and role will be documented, and this policy will be updated before the change is published.

9. Independence and Advertising

Advertising is currently disabled on Textora. If advertising is enabled after approval, ad placements will be separated from editorial content. Ads will not influence which topics are covered, how tools are described, or what the articles say. The site does not publish sponsored content, and it does not accept payment for positive coverage.

10. Reader Feedback

Feedback from readers — corrections, questions, or reports of unclear instructions — improves the site. If a page is confusing or an article misses an important case, that is worth reporting. Use the Contact page or email textora1counter@gmail.com.

11. Changes to This Policy

This editorial policy may be updated if the site's process changes. The effective date at the top of this page indicates the latest version.
