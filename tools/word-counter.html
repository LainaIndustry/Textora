How This Word Counter Works

Words are counted by splitting your text on whitespace. Punctuation attached to a word stays part of that word, so “hello!” is one word. Sentences end at ., ! or ?; paragraphs are separated by blank lines. Unique words are counted after lowercasing and removing most punctuation.

The reading time uses 238 words per minute, and speaking time uses 150 words per minute. Both are estimates. You can adjust the rates on the dedicated Reading Time Calculator and Speaking Time Calculator.

When to Use It

  • Checking an essay or assignment against a word limit.
  • Reviewing post length before publishing.
  • Estimating reading time for a newsletter or article.
  • Spotting unexpected repetition through the unique word count.

Limitations

This is a mechanical counter. It does not perform language-specific tokenization, so languages without space-separated words may be counted differently than expected. Hyphenated compounds are treated as a single word. Emoji and symbols attached to words are not treated as separate words.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does punctuation count as a word?

No. Punctuation attached to a word does not create a separate word. If punctuation is separated by a space, it may briefly be counted as its own token. For most writing, this difference is negligible.

Why does another editor show a slightly different count?

Different tools apply different rules for hyphens, contractions, emoji and non-Latin scripts. Small differences are normal.

Is my text stored?

No. Counting happens locally in your browser. See the Privacy Policy.

Can I set a word count goal?

Yes. Use the optional goal field under the statistics to see a progress bar and percentage.
