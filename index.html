How to Use the Word Counter

A quick walkthrough of the main workflow.

  1. Enter or paste text. Use the editor at the top of this page or open the dedicated Word Counter page.
  2. Review the live counts. Words, characters, sentences, paragraphs and unique words update as you type.
  3. Explore detailed statistics. Reading and speaking times appear in the statistics panel; the dedicated Reading Time Calculator lets you adjust words per minute.
  4. Analyze keyword frequency. Use the keyword panel to see the most common terms and their share of total words.
  5. Format or clean the text. Related tools can remove extra spaces, remove line breaks, or change case.
  6. Copy or download the result. Every tool provides a copy button and most offer a downloadable .txt file.

Statistics are intended to support editing decisions. They are mechanical counts and estimates — not a substitute for human review, and not a guarantee that a text meets a specific publication guideline.

Writing and Text Guidance

Word count versus character count

Word count measures whole tokens separated by whitespace, while character count measures individual code units including spaces and punctuation. Different systems may treat hyphenated words, contractions or emoji differently.

How reading time is estimated

Reading time is estimated by dividing the word count by an assumed words-per-minute rate. We default to 238 WPM. Real reading speed depends on text complexity, reader familiarity and device.

What keyword density means

Keyword density is the number of occurrences of a term divided by the total word count. It is a descriptive metric. Search engines evaluate usefulness and relevance with many signals, and no specific density guarantees a ranking.

Why text formatting matters

Clean whitespace, consistent casing and clear paragraph breaks make text easier to read. Formatting tools help you prepare drafts before they go into a CMS or document editor.

Preparing text for assignments and articles

Check word limits early, review paragraph length, and read the document aloud to catch awkward phrasing. The tools on this site support those checks; they do not replace careful editing.

Cleaning copied text

Text copied from browsers or PDFs often contains extra spaces, broken line breaks or repeated lines. Use the spaces tool and line break tool to tidy it up.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a word counted?

The counter splits your text on whitespace. Any sequence of characters separated by spaces, tabs or line breaks counts as one word. Punctuation attached to a word (for example “hello!”) does not create a separate word. This is a mechanical rule and may not match every language's tokenization.

Are spaces included in character counts?

We show both numbers: characters including spaces, and characters excluding spaces. The second value removes all whitespace characters before counting.

Does the word counter work offline?

Yes. After the page has loaded once, all counting and text processing happens in your browser with JavaScript. No network requests are required for the counting features.

Is my text uploaded to a server?

The counting, formatting and analysis features on this site run locally in your browser. This site does not send the text you type to a server. Hosting providers may still process standard technical logs. See the Privacy Policy for details.

How is reading time estimated?

Word count is divided by an assumed rate of 238 words per minute. You can change that rate on the Reading Time Calculator. Actual reading speed depends on the reader and the material.

Can I download my text?

Yes. The main editor and most tools provide a “Download .txt” button that saves your text as a plain text file.

Does the tool support long documents?

Browser text areas can handle long documents, but very large texts may slow down live statistics in older devices. For extremely long documents, consider splitting the text into sections.

Can I use these tools on a mobile phone?

Yes. All pages are responsive. The mobile menu, editor and tool controls are designed for small screens and touch input.
